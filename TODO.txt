## Medial possessive: "X's Y" where X and Y are two different entities

The clitic trim (`trimSpan` rule 4, src/admit.js) only handles a clitic in LEADING position,
where it is a copula frame ("Name's Brack", "It's Brack"). A clitic in the MIDDLE of a span is
a different thing and is still kept whole:

    Zhukan's Nodachi                  a person, and the weapon they own
    Commander Skywalker's Taun-Taun   a person, and the animal they ride
    Lieutenant Vax's CO               a person, and a DIFFERENT person
    Rangers' Captain Vex              plural possessive — no `s` after the apostrophe at all,
                                      so `CLITIC` cannot even see it

All four are one entity today. They should be two, or one plus a discarded modifier.

Not the same problem as rule 4, and deliberately out of its scope: rule 4 fires on evidence that
the head is a copula frame (never opens a span mid-sentence). These heads DO open spans
mid-sentence — they are real names — so the same test cannot separate them.

Counter-cases the fix must not break, all real, all from the corpus:

    Baldur's Gate (12)      Lord's Alliance (3)      Wayfarer's Suite (2)
    Tower's Dale (1)        Southern Border's Grand Market (1)

These are lexicalised: the possessive is part of the name. 19 occurrences against 7 for the split
class, so a blanket split loses more than it wins.

Ideas, none measured yet:
  - `headTypeOf` on the tail: "Gate"/"Alliance"/"Market" are typed head nouns, "Nodachi"/"CO"
    are not. Covers about half; misses "King's Landing", "Hell's Kitchen", "Rosa's Cantina".
  - Does the left half also stand alone elsewhere in the story? "Zhukan" does, "Baldur" does not.
    Story-wide, so it belongs in `lexis`, not in `spans.js`.
  - Broaden `CLITIC` to plural possessive (`['\u2019]$`) once there is a rule to hand it to.

Related, already fixed by rule 4 as a side effect (see git log): the stray leading title word in
"Lord Lord's Alliance" / "King King's Landing", which still reproduces when the possessive name
is first seen MID-sentence.
